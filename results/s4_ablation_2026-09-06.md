## 2. The gate cannot be evaluated as written, and that is the more important finding

**Median TTD is undefined in both cells, because neither reaches a 50% detection rate.** A
median over 29 sequences of which 12 (or 14) are detected does not exist, so the quantity D10
keyed its decision to does not exist either.

This is not a detail about S4. **It is the project's headline metric.** the project record states
the one-liner as *"optimising median time-to-detection at a fixed budget of 2 alerts per
camera-night — the number fire agencies procure on and nobody publishes."* On DEV, at that
budget, with fitted weights, **that number cannot be computed for PlumeRank or for either
baseline.** It is the first thing a judge would ask for and today the answer is `n/a`.

## 3. On the evidence that does exist, S4 does not earn its place

Substituting detection rate — the only TTD-family quantity that resolves — **S4 makes the
system worse**: it costs two detections (14 → 12) and buys 0.27 minutes of mean time on the
ones that remain. It is not neutral; it is negative.

**But the difference is two sequences out of 29.** That is well inside what this corpus can
resolve, and `data/micro/manifest_micro.json` already records how chaotic detection counts are
here: re-encoding the same frames at JPEG quality 92/95/98/100 moved detections between 0, 2, 2
and 1 of 5. A two-sequence swing is not a measurement.

So the honest position is narrow and it is **not** a defence of S4: **S4 has not earned its
place, and the evidence is too thin to convict it either.** What it has not done, on the only
arm that can speak, is help.

## 4. Recommendation — and why the decision is not taken here

The pre-committed rule says delete. Deleting also removes `cv2.DISOpticalFlow_create` and drops
the defended OpenCV surface count from **23 to 22** (`video` 4 → 3) — a number that appears in
judge-facing documents, and a submission-affecting change. **That is a human decision, not an
agent's**, in the same way the abort branch is.

Recommended order:

1. **Fix the headline first.** A 41% detection rate is the problem behind both this gate and
   §2. Until the median exists, every ablation on this arm is being decided on a substitute
   metric.
2. **Then re-run this cell.** If S4 still costs detections at a detection rate above 50%, the
   D10 rule applies cleanly and `DISOpticalFlow` goes, with the row published either way.
3. **Do not quote 23 surfaces without re-running `check_claims.py`** — the build plan's own
   instruction: *"print what it finds, never what this plan predicted."* It currently finds 23,
   because S4 is still in the code.

# The old fixture rang a bell across the whole frame at ignition

**Date:** 2026-09-06 · **Status: the compositing fix is ADOPTED, and this is what it cost.**
`results/s3_direction_2026-09-06.md` §5.2 recommended this migration and recorded the exact
edit; that edit was applied, reverted the same day as "its own migration", and is now taken
properly, with every test it moves re-calibrated in one pass.

---

## 1. What changed

`tests/test_bench.py::_fire_window` drew a hard ellipse and then blurred the **whole frame**.
It now blurs the **plume** and composites it onto a background that stays sharp.

## 2. The measurement that justifies it, and it is not the one the fix was made for

The fix was made because S3 came out inverted on the fixture (`s3_direction` §2a): the
whole-frame blur softened the background and the reference cloud along with the plume, so the
hard-edged cloud read as *softer* than the soft-edged fire. Correcting the compositing moves
the fire's `raw_s3` from 0.0798 to 0.1897, above the cloud's 0.1733, and S3 points the way it
claims.

Adopting it surfaced a second and larger defect. Measured noise-free, from the last
pre-ignition frame, **outside a generously dilated footprint of the plume itself**:

| frames after t0 | OLD mean abs delta outside | NEW mean abs delta outside | OLD pixels moving >3 | NEW |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | **1.1374** | 0.0293 | 1.37% | 0.14% |
| 2 | 1.1441 | 0.0424 | 1.39% | 0.19% |
| 5 | 1.1546 | 0.0612 | 1.44% | 0.26% |
| 9 | **1.1686** | **0.0875** | 1.50% | 0.36% |

**Under the old fixture, every pixel in the image moved at t0 and stayed moved** — a global
appearance step arriving at exactly the ignition index, thirty-nine times larger than what the
corrected fixture produces. That is the easiest detection in the world and the real corpus
offers nothing like it.

`test_the_fixture_does_not_ring_the_whole_frame_at_ignition` now pins the property rather than
the compositing code: whatever the fixture does, the frame outside the plume must be left
alone.

## 3. What the migration cost, stated plainly

**B2 frame-differencing no longer detects the synthetic fire.** On the old fixture it read
p50 9.0 min; on the corrected one it misses entirely. That is not a regression — it is the
artefact being withdrawn. A frame differencer detects a whole-frame appearance step trivially,
and that step is what it was detecting.

`test_the_offset_shifts_time_to_detection_by_exactly_the_offset` guarded against passing on a
corpus where every system missed, with a floor of 8 comparisons (two systems x four offsets).
Only B1 now detects, so the floor is **4**, with the reason written at the assertion. It is a
fact about the fixture, not a relaxed standard: when PlumeRank detects its own fixture's fire
again, the floor goes back to 8.

## 4. What the migration did NOT fix, and this is the point

**PlumeRank still does not detect the synthetic fire, before or after.** Peak hazards come out
`fire 0.7454` against `cloud 0.7459` — the false alarm still edges the fire, so the budget
threshold solved on the cloud windows sits above the fire's hazard and nothing is ever
promoted.

So the three failures in `tests/test_results_contract.py` **survive this migration unchanged**,
and they are left failing:

```
FAILED tests/test_results_contract.py::test_the_per_system_table_has_a_lane_to_draw
FAILED tests/test_results_contract.py::test_the_operating_curve_has_two_points_per_system
FAILED tests/test_results_contract.py::test_the_sensitivity_strip_has_two_points_to_draw
```

They are correctly reporting the project's largest open defect — the 41.4% detection rate on
DEV and the median that does not exist above it (`SESSION_2026-09-06.md` §4). Making the
fixture's fire easier to find would turn all three green and would be the exact move this
document exists to condemn.

**Suite: 613 passed, 3 failed, 2 skipped.** The three are the three above.
